Input Devices

Input devices are hardware devices used to send data and instructions to a computer. They provide a way for the user to interact with the computer system.

Input devices convert the actions or information provided by the user into signals that the computer can process.

Example: When you press a key on a keyboard, the keyboard sends the corresponding input to the computer. Similarly, clicking a mouse provides an input to the computer.

Keyboard

A keyboard is an input device used to enter letters, numbers and symbols into a computer or other electronic device.

It consists of a set of keys or buttons that allow the user to interact with the computer. A keyboard can be used for typing documents, entering commands, searching for information and performing many other operations.

Types of Keyboards

1. QWERTY Keyboard

The QWERTY keyboard is the most common and widely used keyboard layout.

It is called QWERTY because the first six letters in its top alphabetic row are Q, W, E, R, T and Y.

The layout was inspired by early typewriters. It was designed in the early 1870s by Christopher Latham Sholes.

A QWERTY keyboard contains different groups of keys, including alphanumeric keys, special keys, function keys and modifier keys.

2. Dvorak Keyboard

The Dvorak keyboard is an alternative keyboard layout to QWERTY.

Its keys are arranged to reduce unnecessary finger movement. Because of this arrangement, typing can require less finger movement and can be faster and easier for users who are familiar with the layout.

However, the Dvorak layout did not become as popular as QWERTY because many users found it difficult to adapt to a different arrangement of keys.

3. Ergonomic Keyboard

An ergonomic keyboard is designed to reduce strain and discomfort while typing.

Its design tries to keep the hands and wrists in a more natural position. Some ergonomic keyboards use a split design to provide a more comfortable typing position.

4. Mechanical Keyboard

A mechanical keyboard uses individual mechanical switches or springs under its keys.

These switches make mechanical keyboards durable and also make them easier to repair. They are known for their responsive key presses and distinctive sound.

A major disadvantage is that mechanical keyboards are generally more expensive than standard keyboards.

5. Gaming Keyboard

A gaming keyboard is designed specifically for gaming.

Gaming keyboards may provide quick access to important keys, faster key response, customizable designs and RGB lighting.

They are also designed for durability. Some keyboards may include additional features such as wireless, foldable or backlit designs.

Important Keyboard Keys

A keyboard contains different groups of keys. Each group performs a particular function.

  • Alphanumeric keys: Used for entering letters and numbers.
  • Navigation keys: Include arrow keys, Page Up, Page Down, Home and End.
  • Editing keys: Include Backspace and Delete.
  • Function keys: F1 to F12 are used for different functions.
  • Modifier keys: Shift, Ctrl and Alt are used together with other keys to perform different actions.
  • Enter key: Used to confirm commands or move to a new line.
  • Spacebar: Used to insert a space between characters or words.
  • Esc: Commonly used to cancel or exit an operation.
  • Tab: Used to move to the next field or create indentation.
  • Numeric keypad: Provides a group of number and arithmetic keys.
  • Fn key: Used with other keys to access additional functions on some keyboards.

Mouse

A computer mouse is a small, handheld input device used to control a computer through its graphical user interface (GUI).

A typical mouse has two main buttons and a scroll wheel. It allows the user to perform actions such as clicking, double-clicking, dragging and scrolling.

When the mouse is moved across a surface, its movement is detected and represented by the pointer on the computer screen.

History of the Mouse

The mouse was introduced by Douglas Engelbart in the 1960s.

The early mouse consisted of a wooden box with wheels at the bottom and two buttons on the top. A wire was used to connect it to the computer.

Later, Apple introduced the mouse as an important part of the Macintosh computer. More advanced mouse designs were developed afterwards.

Parts of a Mouse

Mouse Buttons

Mouse buttons are used to perform actions and navigate through the computer interface. The left button is commonly used for clicking and double-clicking, while the right button is commonly used to open additional menus.

Scroll Wheel

The scroll wheel is mainly used to move a page upwards or downwards. It can also perform an action when pressed on some mice.

Motion Detection

Earlier mice used a trackball to detect movement. Modern mice commonly use optical or laser-based methods to detect movement.

Outer Cover or Shell

The outer shell covers and protects the internal components of the mouse.

Types of Mouse

1. Wired Mouse

A wired mouse uses a physical wire to establish a connection with the computer. It is comparatively inexpensive, but the wire can make it less convenient to carry and may become tangled.

2. Wireless Mouse

A wireless mouse communicates with the computer using wireless signals. Bluetooth can be used to establish the connection.

Wireless mice are easier to carry because they do not require a connecting wire, although they may be more expensive and depend on a battery.

3. Mechanical Mouse

A mechanical mouse contains a rubber ball inside it. The ball detects movement when the mouse is moved over a surface.

4. Optical Mouse

An optical mouse uses an LED and photodiodes to detect movement.

It does not use the traditional rolling rubber ball and generally does not require a mouse pad.

5. Laser Mouse

A laser mouse is a type of optical mouse that uses a laser beam to detect movement. Like an optical mouse, it does not use a rolling rubber ball.

6. Trackball Mouse

A trackball mouse has a large trackball in the centre. Instead of moving the whole mouse, the user moves the trackball to control the pointer.

7. Gaming Mouse

A gaming mouse is designed for gaming and usually has additional buttons that allow the user to perform more actions quickly.

Touch Screen

A touch screen is an electronic visual display that can detect the presence and location of a touch, usually from a finger or hand.

It provides an alternative to using a mouse and keyboard for navigating a graphical user interface.

Components of a Touch Screen

1. Touch Sensor

The touch sensor detects a valid touch on the screen. Its structure depends on the type of touch-screen technology being used.

2. Controller

The controller is a small electronic board that determines the location of the touch and provides this information to the device's software.

3. Software Drivers

Software drivers allow the computer's software to communicate and work with the touch screen. The provided material states that these drivers are developed using the C programming language.

Types of Touch Screen Technology

1. Resistive Touch Screen

A resistive touch screen consists of two electrically conducting layers. The outer layer is resistive and the inner layer is conductive.

The two layers are separated by small dots called spacers. When the screen is pressed, the two layers come into contact and the electrical current changes.

The software detects this change and determines the location of the touch.

Resistive touch screens are reliable and durable. However, their multiple layers can make the display harder to read, especially when more light falls on the screen. The material also states that they handle only one touch at a time.

2. Capacitive Touch Screen

A capacitive touch screen detects changes in electrical charge or current when a charged object, such as a person's finger, touches the screen.

Capacitive screens can be of two types:

  • Projective: Uses a tight grid of sensor chips.
  • Surface: Uses sensors at the corners and a thin film distributed over the screen.

When a finger touches the screen, a small amount of electrical charge is transferred. This produces a change that creates a voltage drop at a particular location. The software detects the location and performs the corresponding action.

3. Infrared Touch Screen

An infrared touch screen uses infrared light arranged as a web or grid in front of the display.

When a finger touches the screen, some of the infrared rays are interrupted. The system analyses the location of the interruption and determines where the screen was touched.

4. Surface Acoustic Wave Technology

Surface Acoustic Wave (SAW) technology uses ultrasonic sound waves to detect touch.

The screen produces ultrasonic waves at its edges. These waves travel across the screen and are reflected back.

When a person touches the screen, the waves are disturbed and some of their energy is absorbed by the finger. The system uses this disturbance to determine the location of the touch.

Scanner

A scanner is an electronic input device used to read and convert physical documents, photographs, magazines, posters and other images into digital copies.

These digital copies can then be displayed, edited or stored on a computer. Scanners normally work together with software applications.

Most scanners can be connected to a computer using a cable, while some modern scanners also provide wireless or Bluetooth connectivity.

Working of a Scanner

Although scanner designs may vary, the basic working principle is similar.

  1. The document is placed on the scanner and the lid is closed.
  2. A light source illuminates the document from below.
  3. A scan head containing components such as a mirror, lens, filter and CCD (Charge-Coupled Device) moves across the document.
  4. A belt and stepper motor help move the scan head across the document.
  5. The image is reflected by mirrors and directed towards the lens.
  6. The lens focuses the image onto the CCD array through a filter.
  7. In a three-pass method, red, green and blue filters are used to generate the colour information.
  8. The software combines the resulting colour information to produce a single full-colour digital image.

Key Points to Remember

  • Input devices send data and instructions to a computer.
  • A keyboard is used to enter letters, numbers and symbols.
  • QWERTY is the most common keyboard layout.
  • A mouse is used to control the graphical user interface.
  • A touch screen detects the location of a touch on a display.
  • Major touch technologies covered here are resistive, capacitive, infrared and surface acoustic wave.
  • A scanner converts physical documents and images into digital copies.
