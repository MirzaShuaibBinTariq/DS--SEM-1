Output Devices

An output device is a hardware component that presents the processed information from a computer to the user.

After the computer receives data and processes it, the result needs to be presented in a form that the user can see, read or hear. Output devices perform this function.

Basic computer process:

Input → Processing → Output

What is Output?

Output is the data that has been processed into useful information by the computer.

The output may be displayed on a monitor, printed on paper through a printer, or produced as sound through speakers or headphones.

Hard Copy

A hard copy is the physical form of computer output.

In simple words, if the output can be physically held, it is generally called a hard copy.

Examples of Hard Copy

  • Printed document
  • Printed photograph
  • Printed report
  • Printed chart

Characteristics of Hard Copy

  • It is physical.
  • It is tangible, meaning it can be physically held.
  • It is relatively permanent.
  • It can be carried physically.
  • It is useful for record keeping.

Soft Copy

A soft copy is the electronic form of computer output.

A soft copy exists electronically and can be stored, displayed or played using a computer or another electronic device.

Examples of Soft Copy

  • PDF file
  • Word document
  • Image displayed on a monitor
  • Video
  • Audio recording

Hard Copy vs Soft Copy

Feature Hard Copy Soft Copy
Nature Physical Electronic
Tangible Yes No
Example Printed document PDF
Common device Printer Monitor
Permanence Relatively permanent Can be modified or deleted

Classification of Output Devices

Based on the type of output they produce, output devices can broadly be classified into two groups.

Hard-Copy Output Devices

These devices produce physical output.

  • Printers
  • Plotters

Soft-Copy Output Devices

These devices produce electronic output.

  • Monitor
  • Speakers
  • Headphones

Printer

A printer is an output device that produces information and data from a computer onto paper.

It converts digital information into a physical printed form. Therefore, the output produced by a printer is a hard copy.

Example: When you create a document on a computer and print it on paper, the printer converts the digital document into a physical copy.

Printer Resolution

Resolution refers to the sharpness and clarity of the printed output.

Printer resolution is commonly measured in DPI (Dots Per Inch).

DPI represents approximately how many dots can be represented per inch.

300 DPI → approximately 300 dots per inch

600 DPI → approximately 600 dots per inch

Generally: Higher DPI → finer detail → better print quality

Types of Printers

Printers can be divided into two major categories:

  • Impact Printers
  • Non-Impact Printers

Impact Printers

An impact printer produces output by physically striking a print head or another printing mechanism against an ink ribbon and paper.

Its basic principle is similar to that of a traditional typewriter.

Print Head → Ink Ribbon → Paper

The physical impact creates the printed character.

The main impact printers covered in the material are:

  • Dot Matrix Printer
  • Daisy Wheel Printer
  • Drum Printer

Dot Matrix Printer

A dot matrix printer, also called a wire matrix printer, uses a printing mechanism that creates characters and images using patterns of dots.

It prints one character at a time. Its printing speed is measured in characters per second (CPS).

The speed can vary from about 200 to more than 500 CPS, depending on the printer and print quality.

Print quality depends partly on the number of pins used by the printer. The number of pins can range from 9 to 24.

Most dot matrix printers have a resolution ranging from approximately 72 to 360 DPI.

Advantages and Features

  • Relatively inexpensive.
  • Low operating cost.
  • Can use different fonts and line densities.
  • Can use different types of paper.
  • Many models can print in both directions.
  • Useful where carbon copies are required.

Limitations

  • Generally produces black-and-white output.
  • Print quality is lower than that of many modern printers.
  • Image printing ability is limited.

Dot matrix printers are commonly associated with low-cost applications such as cash registers and situations where carbon copies are needed.

Daisy Wheel Printer

A daisy wheel printer is named after its print head, which resembles a daisy flower. The printing arms look similar to flower petals.

These printers are commonly called letter-quality printers because their printed text can have a quality similar to that of a high-quality typewriter.

Daisy wheel printers produce high-resolution output and are more reliable than dot matrix printers. Their speed can reach up to approximately 90 CPS.

Limitations of Daisy Wheel Printers

  • They mainly produce alphanumeric output.
  • They cannot print graphics.
  • Fonts cannot be changed unless the physical print wheel is replaced.
  • They are relatively slow because the wheel must rotate for each character.
  • They are slower and more expensive than dot matrix printers.

Drum Printer

A drum printer is a type of line printer. Unlike character printers, which print one character at a time, a drum printer can print an entire line in a single operation.

It uses special tractor-fed paper with pre-punched holes along its sides. This arrangement allows continuous high-speed printing.

The printing speed can range from approximately 300 to 2000 lines per minute, depending on the printer.

Features and Limitations

  • Designed for heavy printing applications.
  • Much faster than character printers.
  • Can be quite loud.
  • Has limited multi-font capability.
  • Generally produces lower print quality than many newer printing technologies.
  • Expensive.
  • Character fonts cannot easily be changed.

Non-Impact Printers

A non-impact printer does not physically strike the paper to produce the printed image.

Instead, non-impact printers use methods such as spraying ink or using laser and toner-based processes.

The main non-impact printers covered in the material are:

  • Inkjet Printer
  • Laser Printer

Inkjet Printer

An inkjet printer creates an image by placing extremely small droplets of ink onto paper.

Because it is a non-impact printer, the printing mechanism does not physically strike the paper. Instead, small nozzles spray ink directly onto the paper.

Colour Printing

Modern inkjet printers commonly use four basic colours:

  • C – Cyan
  • M – Magenta
  • Y – Yellow
  • K – Black

This combination is called CMYK.

Inkjet printers can print text, graphics, special characters and images. They typically provide a resolution of around 600 DPI or more.

Features

  • Good-quality text and graphics.
  • Suitable for home users and small offices.
  • Relatively affordable.
  • Can print in different colours.
  • Can print graphics such as charts and graphs.

Inkjet printers normally print at a medium speed, but colour printing can make them slower.

Laser Printer

A laser printer uses a laser and toner-based printing process. It is designed to produce high-quality text and graphics at high speed.

Laser printers are also known as page printers because they process and store the entire page before printing it.

They produce sharp and clear text and graphics. The material gives a resolution range of approximately 300 to 2400 DPI, with 600 DPI being common for many printers.

Features of Laser Printers

  • High-quality text and graphics.
  • Fast printing.
  • Quiet operation.
  • Can print using different fonts and sizes.
  • Suitable for large amounts of printing.

Laser printers are generally faster than inkjet printers, but they are more expensive to purchase and maintain. Their overall cost can include paper, toner replacement and drum replacement.

Impact vs Non-Impact Printers

Feature Impact Printer Non-Impact Printer
Printing method Physically strikes ribbon and paper Does not physically strike paper
Examples Dot Matrix, Daisy Wheel, Drum Inkjet, Laser
Noise Generally noisier Generally quieter
Printing method Mechanical impact Ink spray or laser/toner process

Key Points to Remember

  • Output devices present processed information to the user.
  • Output can be in the form of hard copy or soft copy.
  • A printer converts digital information into physical printed output.
  • Printer resolution is commonly measured in DPI.
  • Impact printers physically strike the ribbon and paper.
  • Dot Matrix, Daisy Wheel and Drum are impact printers.
  • Non-impact printers do not physically strike the paper.
  • Inkjet and Laser printers are non-impact printers.
  • Higher DPI generally means finer detail and better print quality.
