Integer Representation

Computers need a way to represent both positive and negative integers.

Since computers work with binary digits, integers are represented using combinations of 0s and 1s.

Different methods can be used to represent signed integers.

The three important methods are:

  1. Sign and Magnitude
  2. One's Complement
  3. Two's Complement

Sign and Magnitude Representation

In Sign and Magnitude representation, one bit is used to represent the sign of the number, while the remaining bits represent its magnitude or numerical value.

Sign Bit

The first or most significant bit is used as the sign bit.

  • 0 represents a positive number.
  • 1 represents a negative number.

Magnitude

The remaining bits represent the magnitude, or the actual numerical value, of the number.

Example

Consider an 8-bit representation.

0 0000101

Positive 5

Here:

  • 0 is the sign bit, meaning positive.
  • 0000101 represents the magnitude 5.

For the negative value:

1 0000101

Negative 5

The magnitude remains the same, but the sign bit changes from 0 to 1.

One's Complement

In One's Complement representation, a negative number is obtained by changing every bit of the positive number.

In other words:

0 → 1      1 → 0

Example

Suppose the positive number is represented as:

00000101

To obtain its One's Complement, change every 0 to 1 and every 1 to 0.

11111010

Therefore, the One's Complement is obtained by inverting all the bits.

Important Point

One's Complement has two representations of zero:

  • Positive zero
  • Negative zero

Two's Complement

Two's Complement is another method of representing signed integers.

To find the Two's Complement of a binary number:

  1. Find the One's Complement by changing every 0 to 1 and every 1 to 0.
  2. Add 1 to the result.

Two's Complement
= One's Complement + 1

Example

Consider the binary representation:

00000101

Step 1: Find the One's Complement.

00000101
↓
11111010

Step 2: Add 1.

11111010
+ 00000001
11111011

Therefore, the Two's Complement is:

11111011

Sign and Magnitude vs One's Complement

Feature Sign and Magnitude One's Complement
Basic method One bit represents sign and remaining bits represent magnitude Negative value is obtained by inverting every bit
Positive number Sign bit 0 with magnitude Normal binary representation
Negative number Sign bit becomes 1 All bits are inverted
Zero Has positive and negative zero Has positive and negative zero

One's Complement vs Two's Complement

Feature One's Complement Two's Complement
Method Invert every bit Invert every bit and add 1
Operation 0 becomes 1 and 1 becomes 0 One's Complement + 1
Zero Two representations of zero One representation of zero

Comparison of Integer Representations

Method Main Idea Negative Number Zero
Sign & Magnitude Separate sign bit and magnitude Change the sign bit to 1 Two zeros
One's Complement Invert all bits Change 0 → 1 and 1 → 0 Two zeros
Two's Complement Invert all bits and add 1 One's Complement + 1 One zero

Quick Revision

  • Signed integers can represent both positive and negative numbers.
  • In Sign and Magnitude, one bit represents the sign and the remaining bits represent magnitude.
  • In Sign and Magnitude, 0 represents positive and 1 represents negative.
  • One's Complement is obtained by changing every 0 to 1 and every 1 to 0.
  • Two's Complement is obtained by taking the One's Complement and adding 1.
  • Sign and Magnitude and One's Complement have two representations of zero.
  • Two's Complement has one representation of zero.
